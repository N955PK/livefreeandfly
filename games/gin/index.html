House rules
  • Ten cards each. One card is turned up: it starts the discard pile and sets the knock. A face card means 10, an ace means 1.
  • The non-dealer may take the turned-up card or pass; then the dealer may. If both pass, the non-dealer draws from the deck.
  • Go down any time your deadwood is at or under the knock. No deadwood is gin.
  • After a knock the other player lays off on the knocker’s melds. Nothing can be laid off on gin.
  • Gin scores 25 plus the other player’s deadwood. A knock scores the difference.
  • Undercut: if the other player ties or beats the knocker after laying off, they score 25 plus the difference.
  • Settling up: when a game ends the winner adds 100 for the game and each player adds 25 for every hand they won (a box). The loser pays the difference, a penny a point. If the loser never scored, the game bonus is doubled. Whoever opens the table can change every one of these figures.
  • Hollywood: your first winning hand counts in game one, your second in games one and two, the rest in all three.
  • You may throw back the card you just took off the pile.
  • Two cards left in the deck and nobody down: dead hand, no score. The winner of a hand deals the next, so the loser gets first look at the turned-up card.
